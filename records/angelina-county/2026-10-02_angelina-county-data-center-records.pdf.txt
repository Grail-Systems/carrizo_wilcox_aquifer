SUPPORTING THE LIMITED REGULATORY AUTHORITY OF
COUNTIES IN RURAL'AREAS ON EMERGING TECHNOLOGIES
~
INCLUDING DATA CENTERS AND BATTERY STORAGE FACILITIES
WHEREAS, Texas is a premier destination for innovation and our counties have long welcomed the
,
tax base and economic growth that accompany digital and energy infrastructure; and
,
WHEREAS, The pace of data center and battery storage facility construction has exponentially
increased across the United States and within the State ofTexas;and
 ~
WHEREAS, local concerns have emerged across Texas regarding the potential adverse impacts of
i
- large-scale data center developments on surrounding communities, the environment, public health,
consumer utility costs, land use compatibility, and strain on local infrastructure and resources; and
WHEREAS, the scale of emerging technologies has shifted from modest facilities to "gigawatt-scale"
:
campuses, creating a regulatory gap at the county level that has become a significant liability for
residents; and
.
.
;
‘
WHEREAS, counties have nosignificant land use authority; and
,
WHEREAS, the 24/7 operation oflarge-scale cooling fans and backup generators creates low-
frequency noise pollution, while large-scale battery installations present unique public safety,
challenges, including specialized emergency response requirements; impacting the health and
property values of neighboring families; and
;
WHEREAS, a single data center facility can consume millions of gallons ofwater per day for
evaporative cooling, and the potential for chemical runoff (in the event of a fire) from battery sites
threatens local aquifers and utility resources in counties already managing depleted groundwater
levels; and
°
;
‘
.
WHEREAS, the frequent use of non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) prevents public transparency,
leading to community distrust and unnecessary litigation when residents learn of massive projects
only after permits are issued;
.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the Angelina County Commissioners Court requests that
the Texas Legislature grant counties limited and specific authority to regulate emerging
technologies including data center and battery energy storage developments including but not
limited to the following:
.
1.
Reasonable Buffer Zones & Noise Mitigation: Authority to mandate minimum setback
requirements and sound-attenuation standards for facilities including battery enclosures
and cooling units adjacent to residential or agricultural zones to protect the "quiet
.
enjoyment" ofTexas homes. Reasonable noise limitations and limits at the property line.
The use of low-noise chiller systems and sound attenuated generator enclosures.
2.
Light pollution standards based on the International Dark Sky standards to limit glare and
:
3. Water Availability & Resource Protection: Authority to require a certified Water Impact
.
i
wot
Statement and Environmental Safety Plan for high-load industrial users, granting counties
-
the standing to negotiate water usage limits, alternative cooling technologies, or
:
containment strategies for hazardous materials if a project threatens local water security,
:
soil health, or agriculture sustainability.
.
4,
° Mandatory Public Hearing Process: Requirement for a public hearing process for any data
center or battery storage facility exceeding a specific megawatt (MW) or megawatt-hour
:
(MWh) threshold to ensure community engagement, coordination with local first
:
responders, and a predictable path for development.
:
.
5.
Construction techniques and materials to reduce thermal footprint and heat emissions.
7
bs
6.
Professionally designed landscape plan; tree canopy and vegetative screening.
7.
Cooling systems, generators, and electrical equipment to be fully enclosed and/or visually
i
screened.
.
NOW THEREFORE BEIT RESOLVED that the Angelina County Commissioners Court supports limited
regulatory authority ofcounties in rural area on emerging technologies including data centers and battery
a
storage facilities in order to reduce potential impacts on surrounding communities and neighborhoods, the
environment, public health, land use compatibility and strain on local infrastructure and resources.

RESOLUTION 010-26
SUPPORTING THE LIMITED REGULATORY AUTHORITY OF.
COUNTIES IN RURAL’AREAS ON EMERGING TECHNOLOGIES
—
INCLUDING DATA CENTERS AND BATTERY STORAGE FACILITIES
.
°. WHEREAS, Texas is a premier destination for innovation and our counties have long welcomed the.
tax base and economic growth that accompany digital and energy infrastructure; and
;
;
WHEREAS, The pace of data center and battery storage facility construction has exponentially
increased across the United States and within the State of Texas; and
WHEREAS, local concerns have emerged across Texas regarding the potential adverse impacts of
i
- large-scale data center developments on surrounding communities, the environment, public health,
consumer utility costs, land use compatibility, and strain on local infrastructure and resources; and
WHEREAS, the scale of emerging technologies has shifted from modest facilities to "gigawatt-scale"
:
campuses, creating a regulatory gap at the county level that has becomea significant liability for
.
residents; and
.
.
:
:
,
WHEREAS, counties have no significant land use authority;and
‘
WHEREAS, the 24/7 operation oflarge-scale cooling fans and backup generators creates low-
frequency noise pollution, while large-scale battery installations present unique public safety,
challenges, including specialized emergency response requirements; impacting the health and
property values of neighboring families; and
;
WHEREAS, a single data center facility can consume millions of gallons ofwater per day for
evaporative cooling, and the potential for chemical runoff (in the event of a fire) from battery sites
threatens local aquifers and utility resources in counties already managing depleted groundwater
levels; and
°
‘
:
oe
WHEREAS, the frequent use of non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) prevents public transparency,
leading to community distrust and unnecessary litigation when residents learn of massive projects
;
;
only after permits are issued;
.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the Angelina County Commissioners Court requests that
the Texas Legislature grant counties limited and specific authority to regulate emerging
i
.
technologies including data center and battery energy storage developments incliiding but not
limited to the following:
:
.
|
1.
Reasonable Buffer Zones & Noise Mitigation: Authority to mandate minimum setback
:
.
requirements and sound-attenuation standards for facilities including battery enclosures
:
and cooling units adjacent to residential or agricultural zones to protect
the "quiet
.
'
enjoyment" ofTexas homes. Reasonable noise limitations and limits at the property line.
\
;
The use of low-noise chiller systems and sound attenuated generator enclosures.
i
2.
Light pollution standards based on the International Dark Sky standards to limit glare and
.
:..
3. Water Availability & Resource Protection: Authority to require a certified Water Impact...
ot
Statement and Environmental Safety Plan for high-load industrial users, granting counties
;
a
the standing to negotiate water usage limits, alternative cooling technologies, or
.
:
containment strategies for hazardous materials if a project threatens local water security,
:
soil health, or agriculture sustainability.
.“
4,° Mandatory Public Hearing Process: Requirement for a public hearing process for any data
:
center or battery storage facility exceeding a specific megawatt (MW) or megawatt-hour
:
(MWh) threshold to ensure community engagement, coordination with local first
;
responders, and a predictable path for development.
:
.
5.
Construction techniques and materials to reduce thermal footprint and heatemissions.
==
*
-
6.
Professionally designed landscape plan; tree canopy and vegetative screening.
7.
Cooling systems, generators, and electrical equipment to be fully enclosed and/or visually
i
screened.
.
.
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Angelina County Commissioners Court supports limited
.
regulatory authority of counties in rural area on emerging technologies including data centers and battery
aa
storage facilities in order to reduce potential impacts on surrounding communities and neighborhoods, the

To Whom It May Concern:
The undersigned elected officials express our full support for EPG Champion’s planned
redevelopment of the former Abitibi Paper Mill site in Lufkin, Texas, into a state-of-the-art
data center and on-site power generation facility.
This site, located within a federally designated Opportunity Zone, represents one of the
most economically distressed areas of our region. For nearly two decades, it has stood as a
reminder of the jobs and economic vitality our community lost when the paper mill closed.
The proposed project represents a once-in-a-generation opportunity to restore that
economic strength—bringing high-quality jobs, substantial tax revenue, and new
infrastructure investment to the Angelina County area.
We believe this project aligns with the long-term vision our community has shared for the
site: revitalization, responsible industrial use, and sustainable economic growth. EPG
Champion’s investment will not only create direct employment opportunities during
construction and operation but will also support local suppliers, contractors, and small
businesses across the region.
We strongly encourage all stakeholders to give this project the attention and priority it
deserves. Its success will mark a major step forward for East Texas and serve as a model
for how legacy industrial sites can be reimagined for modern, technology-driven industries.

To Whom It May Concern:
The undersigned elected officials express our full support for EPG Champion’s planned
redevelopment of the former Abitibi Paper Mill site in Lufkin, Texas, into a state-of-the-art
data center and on-site power generation facility.
This site, located within a federally designated Opportunity Zone, represents one of the
most economically distressed areas of our region. For nearly two decades, it has stood asa
reminder of the jobs and economic vitality our community lost when the paper mill closed.
The proposed project represents a once-in-a-generation opportunity to restore that
economic strength—bringing high-quality jobs, substantial
tax revenue, and new
infrastructure investment to the Angelina County area.
We believe this project aligns with the long-term vision our community has shared for the
site: revitalization, responsible industrial use, and sustainable economic growth. EPG
Champion’s investment will not only create direct employment opportunities during
construction and operation but will also support local suppliers, contractors, and small
businesses across the region.
We strongly encourage all stakeholders to give this project the attention and priority it
deserves. Its success will mark a major step forward for East Texas and serve as a model
for how legacy industrial sites can be reimagined for modern, technology-driven industries.

Project Status Update
We appreciate your continued support in advancing our large-load application with Oncor and helping
position this project for success in East Texas.
During our last discussion with Oncor in late 2025, the expected power delivery timeline and cost
structure were as follows:
¢75 MW available in 2026
+ Full 245 MW available in 2027-2028
- Estimated utility upgrade cost: $15-$20 million (via letter of credit or cash)
Following a recent call with Oncor, these assumptions have changed materially:
«20 MW available in 2026
« Balance of 245 MW now projected for 2029-2030
« Updated utility upgrade requirement: approximately $226 million
Oncor has indicated that the underlying cause is that the local transmission system is currently
underbuilt and cannot support significant new load. As a result, this project would fund substantial
infrastructure improvements, including:
1

« Over 80 miles of transmission upgrades
« Multiple substation expansions and enhancements
Despite this significant increase in cost and extended timeline, our investors and partners remain
committed and have indicated their ability to meet these requirements.
Oncor has formally approved the project on its side, and we have accepted the study results. The project
is now under review by ERCOTfor final approval.
ERCOT Process and Urgency
ERCOT approvalis the final step. Historically, ERCOT has largely validated utility findings; however, we
understand the current process has slowed, and a new “batch study” process is scheduled to begin
July 15, 2026.
\f this project is delayed and pushed into that batch process, it will materially jeopardize—and
likely
terminate—the project due to timing and uncertainty.
Strategic Enhancements to the Project
To further strengthen both the project and the regional grid, we have:
Partnered with a major publicly traded company to develop 300 MW of on-site generation
« Engaged with a separate developer advancing a 600 MW power plant between Lufkin and
Nacogdoches (currently in ERCOT’s interconnection queue)
These initiatives are expected to:
e Strengthen the local transmission system
« improve regional grid reliability and resiliency
e Attract additional generation to East Texas
Regional and Economic Importance
This project represents a unique opportunity for East Texas:
«itis located in an economically distressed area, including an Opportunity Zone
+ ithas strong local community and leadership support
« it will generate outsized tax revenue and economic impact relative to typical urban data center
developments

2

East Texas share in this investment and economic benefit.
