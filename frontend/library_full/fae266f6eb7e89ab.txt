Powered Land: How East Texas Water Is Being Committed Out of Public View Oct 15, 2026 (updated Nov. 24, 2026) · East Texas Legacy Coalition · @Fawn Elizabeth Zion Angelina County residents keep hearing the same number: about 500 gallons of water a day, roughly three houses. They heard it about the Amp Z data center at the old paper mill. A second data center developer told the groundwater district the same thing. The records tell a different story. That second developer asked for 13 million gallons a year. A power plant planned north of Lufkin asked for 1.5 billion. The city that owns the mill's wells says it won't cap the water it sells, told us in February it had "no responsive documents available for release" about Amp Z's utility needs, and is now asking the Texas Attorney General whether it can withhold records about the project. This piece pulls together records we obtained through public information requests to the county, the city and the Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District, the reporting of the Lufkin Daily News and The 936, and officials' own words on camera. Documented facts say where they come from. Paragraphs that begin "Our read" are our conclusions from those facts. We are not claiming anyone was bribed, and we are not speculating about any criminal case. We are laying out how decisions about our water and land have been made, and asking the questions the record leaves open. It sits just outside Lufkin city limits, so the city has no zoning or building permit authority over it, and the county has almost none. The property is owned by Verdant Industries and Verdant Industries Realty. The Lufkin Daily News reported that corporate records identify Eric Estes as president of both companies. On camera, Wright said the developers told him they had already spent money on upgrades to bring initial power to the site. The land was not sitting empty. In a December 15, 2025 email to Commissioner Kenneth Jeffrey, County Judge Keith Wright answered a question about Amp Z: "They have the crypo [sic] mining going on. They [have] a third party marketing the land for the data center." The email does not make clear whether "they" means Amp Z or the landowner. Amp Z Champion Data Center Holdings, a Denver-based company, does not own the land. The Lufkin Daily News reported that it holds an option to buy about 1,041 acres from Verdant. In October 2026, Wright described the company as owning land on both sides of Twin Oaks Road. Whether Amp Z has exercised its option is not clear from public reporting. Building the package Before any official signed anything, the pieces that make an industrial site valuable to a data center buyer were already being assembled: an air permit, a power connection and an assurance of water. The air permit. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality issued Verdant an air permit in January 2025 for a power plant with 12 natural gas turbines. The Lufkin Daily News found that the permit's annual particulate estimate was first put at 249.9976 tons, about 4.8 pounds below the 250-ton line that would have triggered a federal Prevention of Significant Deterioration review, with deeper air analysis and more public involvement. Later reporting by the paper showed that on Dec. 23, 2024, a TCEQ reviewer asked whether the company could bring the figure under 249.99 tons when rounded. The revised estimate was 249.9947 tons, about 10.6 pounds under the line, and TCEQ approved the permit in January 2025. The water. After a September 2025 meeting at the city's economic development offices, Ryan Smith of Amp Z asked then-Lufkin City Manager Kevin Gee how the company could "discretely speak to potential customers about the water situation in Lufkin," the paper reported. Gee, who was also president of the Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District, sent the company a water-availability letter on Sept. 25, 2025, two days after Smith's Sept. 23 email (City of Lufkin records, CRR-2026-315 Item 11). The engineering. County emails show the Savannah engineering firm Thomas & Hutton invited Wright to a September 30, 2025 meeting with Amp Z executives and the project's engineers, and was working with him on site access by February 2026: on Feb. 11, its project manager asked about an access point off Twin Oaks Road, and on March 3 Wright replied that the county was agreeable but that there would need to be a road use agreement. Our read: each of these pieces raises what the site is worth to whoever ultimately builds on it. Wright's description of "a third party marketing the land" fits that picture. The endorsements The last piece was political support, and it was requested in writing. On November 7, 2025, Mayor Mark Hicks hosted a meeting at the former Temple-Inland boardroom in Diboll. His invitation listed the Estes Family Office, Amp Z executives, Wright, state Rep. Trent Ashby, state Sen. Robert Nichols, U.S. Rep. Pete Sessions, Angelina College President Michael Simon, Lufkin ISD Superintendent James Hockenberry and Lufkin EDC Director Larissa Philpot-Brown. The agenda included "Discussion of utility/Oncor-related issues" and "Brainstorming where and how we can best utilize assistance." According to Hicks, Wright posted a group photo on Facebook that day: "Working together to bring a major opportunity to Lufkin and Angelina County! Stay tuned." Five days later, Raine Cotton of EPG Champion Development, also an Amp Z governing person, emailed the attendees a form letter. It declared "full support" for turning the mill into "a state-of-the-art data center and on-site power generation facility." Cotton wrote that he would compile every signature "into a single version for submission." The letter is addressed "To Whom It May Concern." Nobody has said where it was submitted. According to the Lufkin Daily News, Councilman Robert Shankle signed it November 17, Hicks signed it on city letterhead the next day despite a city policy against business endorsements, and Hockenberry and Simon confirmed signing. Nichols said he did not recall signing, and House candidate Rocky Thigpen said he took no position. Wright returned his on November 18 with two words: "See attached." Our records request shows his copy was signed "Angelina County Judge" and stamped with the official seal of Angelina County. The template Cotton sent had no seal. When the paper asked in September 2026 whether he had signed, Wright did not say. In a later interview with The 936, he acknowledged it: "Yes, we supported it. I supported it." He said he had been given a two-hour window to respond and could not find the letter. We reviewed the Commissioners Court's minutes for Nov. 12, 17, 21 and 25 and Dec. 9, 2025, and found no vote to endorse the project. Managing the message The Lufkin Daily News broke the story in February 2026. By then, several officials had been engaged for months and had signed letters of support. What followed was a managed rollout, documented in records obtained by the paper. •​ The news release. On February 5, Philpot-Brown sent city leaders a draft release and noted, "I sent this to AmpZ for review." It repeated the developer's projections for investment, jobs and water use. •​ The talking points. The next morning, Feb. 6, at 9:33 a.m., hours before Wright's State of the County address, she emailed Wright and city staff a draft for talking points: "Not my best work, but it's the best ChatGPT and I could do in ten minutes." •​ The water number. The city's FAQ repeated Amp Z's estimate of about 500 gallons a day after an initial fill, compared to three homes. The paper reported that number came from the developer, was not independently verified, and did not address the water the 12 gas turbines could use. Gee later told the paper that large users could draw as much water as the system can deliver, as long as they pay. •​ Asking Austin to stay out. On February 9, Philpot-Brown sent Gee, Bray, Hicks and Wright "Chamber Days Talking Points" for meetings with Ashby and Nichols' staff. One line urged lawmakers "to resist efforts to get between data center developments and the cities and counties that serve them." When Councilman Jimmy Ford asked basic questions about the cooling system's water use, noise and emissions, Philpot-Brown replied that she would forward them "to AmpZ to get their answers directly." Meanwhile, the record undercuts the reassurances. Verdant's TCEQ air permit application states "No water balance is possible." Amp Z's county tax abatement application, filed in March 2026, listed water, wastewater, electric and gas needs as "TBD" and checked "No" for environmental issues needing to be addressed. The water question The City of Lufkin owns the former mill's wells, which it bought in 2009. It is the one local government with a real lever over this project: whether, and how much, water to sell. What residents have been told about that lever does not line up. What a city official told us privately. On February 17, 2026, a City of Lufkin economic development official told ETLC's founder that the site could supply about 20 million gallons, but that the city was capping the project's water, and that if the project tried to use more than it had stated, it would have to apply for new permits. The official also said the city had plans in place so the project could not drain the aquifers. This is our founder's firsthand account of that conversation. Separately, Mayor Hicks told The 936 on camera that the paper mill used about 20 million gallons of water a day when it operated. We are not naming the official, who appeared to be trying to protect residents. What city leaders said on the record. Then-City Manager Kevin Gee told the Lufkin Daily News the city understood from Amp Z that it would need roughly 500 gallons a day after a large initial fill, and that large users would be treated like any other customer and could draw as much water as the system can deliver, as long as they pay. Mayor Hicks told The 936: "We didn't limit any type of water usage by a user. We don't do that with anyone." What the city said about its records. On February 17, 2026, the same day, ETLC asked the city for any utility agreements, impact studies, and communications with Amp Z about utility capacity. Two days later the City Secretary replied that "after conducting a reasonable search," there were "no responsive documents available for release at this time," and cited the economic development exception. In October 2026, answering a later ETLC request, the city said "certain potentially responsive records" had been sent to the Texas Attorney General for a ruling on whether they can be withheld. The same day, Oct. 9, the city referred records from a second ETLC request to the Attorney General as well. City leaders have also been open about wanting a large water customer. In February, Hicks told the Lufkin Daily News that "for years, people have talked about, 'We've got to find somebody to come in here that can use our water.'" In the 936 interview, the officials described selling water to industry as "a profit center for the city." Our read: either a written cap and an aquifer protection plan exist, or residents were given a reassurance with nothing behind it. The city now holds records about this project that it has asked the Attorney General for permission to withhold. We have asked the city for any document setting a water cap or aquifer protection plan, and for a copy of its request to the Attorney General. Beyond Amp Z: the other draws on the same water Amp Z is not the only project. Records ETLC obtained from the Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District show how water for this wave of development is being lined up across the county, and how often the public is kept out of it. Amp Z has filed nothing with the groundwater district. On September 22, 2026, District General Manager Wil Blough wrote that the district had "not been in contact" with Amp Z or Verdant, had received no well permit applications near the mill in two years, and had "not been involved in any talks with this entity over groundwater use." The wells near the property belong to the City of Lufkin or other industries. The district said none of those well owners has asked to increase its permitted pumping. That means Amp Z's water, whatever the amount, runs through the city's existing wells, with no new groundwater permit and no district hearing. HyperDataGrid: "about 500 gallons per day" and a 13-million-gallon request. The developers of the second data center site, on Ben Dunn Road off Highway 103 West, bought a 187-acre tract with five water wells through Paluxy LandCo. In an August 5, 2026 email, Blough wrote to developer Clay Roberts that he had said "you did not think you would be using more than about 500 gallons per day." Three of the wells previously held permits totaling 17 million gallons a year. Blough then explained the thresholds: "If you are wanting to keep all 5 and not have a board meeting/public hearing, the cumulative permit total for all five wells needs to be under 15 million gallons per year and pumps cannot be over 69 gpm." Pumps under 17.3 gallons per minute "would not need permits." By September, the developers had asked by phone and email for a lower total of 13 million gallons a year. The district then issued three permits, A0046, A0047 and A0048, for 3, 5 and 5 million gallons a year, 13 million in all, dated Oct. 12, 2026. Blough wrote on Nov. 23 that they were approved at the staff level, with no board vote, because the combined volume was under the 15-million-gallon threshold. His emails show the application check arrived Oct. 16 and the permits were still being prepared the week of Oct. 19. Five hundred gallons a day is about 182,500 gallons a year. Thirteen million is roughly 70 times that, and sits just under the line that would require a public hearing. A power plant asking for 1.5 billion gallons a year. Blough also disclosed that WattBridge was approved for 80 million gallons a year (two permits of 40 million each) for a power plant north of Lufkin. The district board approved them on Feb. 10, 2025, with no public comment, and the wells were never drilled. In March 2026 the company changed the plant from peaking to continuous 24/7 operation and requested 1.5 billion gallons a year, about 4.1 million gallons a day. The project went on hold again in July 2026 at the company's request. Separately, Judge Wright said on camera that a power plant the county gave a tax abatement is "talking about producing 1.2 gigawatts" and supplying data centers directly. We have not confirmed whether that plant and WattBridge are the same project. ERCOT's October 2026 interconnection report lists a second 600-megawatt gas plant, Longleaf II (project 30INR0114, WattBridge Texas Holdings), planned for July 2030. The first Longleaf project (26INR0294) does not appear in the rows we pull from that report; we have not confirmed why. Our read: the public keeps hearing "500 gallons a day," project by project. The records show the larger volumes sit elsewhere: in city wells the city says it won't cap, in well fields sized just under the hearing trigger, and in the power plants built to feed this load. Margaret Cook of the Houston Advanced Research Center has warned against cooling choices that end up "moving the water use upstream to your power plant," and has called for a measure that counts the water used by a data center and its power source together. Nobody is adding these together. The turn The public didn't accept the managed version. Residents rallied at Lufkin City Hall in May 2026 and packed the Commissioners Court in June. In an online Lufkin Daily News reader poll, which was not a scientific survey, 79% opposed the project, according to the paper's opinion contributor Sylvia Weathers. Officials repositioned: •​ The money went back. The Estes contributions to Wright, Ashby, Thigpen and Skillern were returned in late January 2026, after the indictment. •​ The abatement stalled. Amp Z's consultant, KE Andrews, filed for a ten-year abatement in March 2026: 100% in the first year, stepping down 10 points a year. Wright wrote back asking for job, wage, local-spending and conservative water figures. The county later said the application had been rejected and no new one had been submitted. •​ The resolution. On July 14, 2026, the Commissioners Court unanimously passed Resolution 010-26, asking the Legislature for authority over setbacks, noise, lighting and water impact statements. Its own text warns that a single data center "can consume millions of gallons of water per day" and that NDAs leave residents learning of projects "only after permits are issued." •​ The words softened. Greg Weatherbee of The 936 reminded Wright that at his February State of the County address he had said data centers are coming and people need to accept it. Wright said he had been misunderstood and was talking about global competition, and that his "position has evolved" and he is "more concerned about the impacts of data centers." We credit that shift. Wright and the court did more than most counties have. But some public statements still don't match the record. In August, the Lufkin Daily News reported that Wright reassured Rotarians data centers' water needs should not be a concern, weeks after signing a resolution warning the opposite. On camera, Hicks said that in November 2025 "no one was talking about data centers," though the form letter he was sent and signed that month calls the project "a state-of-the-art data center" in its first sentence. Where it stands Amp Z has gone quiet. It has not submitted a new abatement application. In August, Gov. Greg Abbott directed the PUC and ERCOT to hold off approving new data center grid connections pending an audit. In September, he ordered the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality to pause all data center permits until that audit is finished, and said the next legislative session will work to eliminate financial incentives for data centers. On Nov. 3, 2026, Abbott lost his race for a fourth term to Democrat Gina Hinojosa, who takes office in January. A second project, HyperDataGrid, is moving faster on Highway 103 West, and Hicks said on camera it will use wells on its own site. The county's remaining leverage is a road-use agreement. On October 13, 2026, commissioners hired attorney Bruce Green for $1,500 to draft one. Wright told KLTV the company wants to close part of a nearby road, which would limit access from Twin Oaks Mobile Home Park to Highway 103. In a March 26, 2026 email to Amp Z's tax consultant, Wright asked for a town hall before any Commissioners Court vote, and the Lufkin Daily News reported in October that the expectation still stands. At the June 9 Commissioners Court meeting, Wright said at least two public hearings would be held before any deal is finalized, and that the county would not bring a tax abatement agreement to the court unless Amp Z first held a public meeting (KLTV, June 9, 2026). Kevin Gee's resignation as city manager was accepted Nov. 9, 2026. On Nov. 10, commissioners voted 4–0 to retain lawyers to help develop an Economic Development Agreement under Chapter 312 of the Tax Code and Chapter 381 of the Local Government Code; the minutes name no project and no law firm. Our read: the fight does not end if Amp Z walks away. The turbine permit, the substation, the water letter and the political goodwill stay with the land. Commissioners Court agendas and minutes What we're asking To every official who signed the letter, and to Judge Wright first among them: 1.​ Withdraw the letters in writing, to the same people you sent them to. Wright said any commitment would require a Commissioners Court vote. The court's minutes for Nov. 12 through Dec. 9, 2025 show no such vote, yet his letter carries the county seal. 2.​ Say where the letters went. If they were submitted to ERCOT, Oncor, investors or a state program, the public deserves to know. 3.​ Release the December 11 "framework" and every record of communication with Amp Z, Verdant and EPG Champion, including texts on personal phones. Under the Texas Public Information Act, those are public records. 4.​ Correct the record on the campaign contribution dates. 5.​ Hold the hearings. Before any road-use agreement or incentive is approved, hold the public hearings Wright promised, and require a cleanup bond, water reporting and noise limits in writing. To Sen.-elect Trent Ashby, elected Nov. 3 to succeed retiring Sen. Robert Nichols, and to Rep.-elect Rocky Thigpen, elected Nov. 3 to Ashby's House seat: Before he lost reelection, Gov.
